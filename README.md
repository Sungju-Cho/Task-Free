### Why it is built this way

**The first sentence is the product list, not the company history.** Distributors triage on a phone,
and the preview pane shows roughly the first 90 characters. If those characters are "We are a Korean
manufacturer established in 1990," the reader learns nothing about whether this concerns them. If
they are "We manufacture PMMA bone cement, 100% beta-TCP...", the reader knows in one second.

**No bullet list.** Bullets look like a brochure, and a brochure reads as bulk mail. Two sentences
in a row carry the same information and look like a person typed them.

**The company credentials come last in that sentence,** where they act as reassurance rather than as
the opening claim.

**The ask is the lowest-commitment version possible.** "Come by, no appointment needed" is easier to
agree to than "would 30 minutes work" - and once they are at the stand, the booth script takes over.

## 2b. Sending mechanics

- **Send from your company mail client**, not from a connected personal Gmail. The from-address has
  to read `jasoncho@meta-biomed.com` or the email is judged before it is opened.
- **BCC, never To or CC.** Putting 60 competitors' addresses in one visible header is the single
  most damaging thing you can do here.
- **20-30 per batch, spread across days.** One 200-recipient send looks like bulk mail to filters.
- **No attachments on the first email.** They hurt deliverability and the link does the same job.
- **Timing.** Most recipients are UTC+7 or +8. Send 10:00-11:00 KST, Tuesday to Thursday, and it
  lands first thing in their morning.
- **Watch Cloudflare Web Analytics after each batch.** A visit spike means the batch reached
  inboxes. Flat means it went to spam - change the subject line before sending the next batch.
- **Deadline.** Today is 21 August; the show opens 9 September. First batch out by 25 August so the
  follow-up still lands before people fix their week.

## 3. Booth script

Nothing to fill in. Read it twice before the hall opens.

### Before anyone arrives

Stand up, and stand beside the panel rather than in front of the entrance. Do not sit, do not eat at
the stand, do not work on your laptop. A visitor decides whether to step in from three metres away,
and a seated exhibitor reads as closed.

Have ready: samples out of the box and in reach, IFUs in a folder, the page open on your phone at
the shortlist section, and a pen.

### Opening

Never open with "How can I help you?" - the answer is always "just looking."

> "Good morning. What are you sourcing at the moment - spine, sports medicine, or general surgery?"

One question, three doors. Whatever they answer routes the rest of the conversation, and it takes
four seconds to find out whether this person is worth twenty minutes.

If they say "just looking":

> "Of course. We are a Korean manufacturer - bone cement, bone graft substitute, suture anchors and
> ligating clips. If any of those are in your range, I am here all three days."

Then step back. Do not follow them.

### Branches - use only the one that matches

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| Spine / vertebroplasty | "OpaCem - PMMA cement for vertebral augmentation, 60% zirconium dioxide. Here is the IFU and the handling data. What are you using now?" |
| Knee / hip arthroplasty | "NTCem - 4.22% gentamicin sulfate in the powder, violet pigment, eight-minute set at 21 degrees. Are you supplying cemented or cementless mostly?" |
| Sports medicine | "Four anchors in one system - PEEK-OPTIMA medial row, two knotless lateral options, and an all-suture anchor. One supplier for the whole construct instead of two." |
| Bone graft | "SurgyBone, 100% beta-TCP. Wedges for HTO, cylinders for revision knee, chips and granules. Which shapes move in your market?" |
| Laparoscopy / general surgery | "MetaClip-P - polyoxymethylene ligating clips, four sizes from 2 to 16 millimetres, with reusable appliers. Here are the REF numbers and pack quantities." |

Always end your branch with a question back. A monologue ends the conversation; a question keeps
them at the stand.

### Objections - answer honestly, then move

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We already have a supplier." | "Understood. Most people who sign with us are adding a second source, not replacing one - usually because their current supplier cannot move on documentation or on OEM. If that ever becomes a problem, keep my card." |
| "Are you registered in my country?" | "Not yet. That is exactly why I am here - we are looking for a partner to hold the registration. We supply CE, ISO 13485, KGMP, Free Sale Certificate and Certificate of Origin, so the filing does not start from zero." |
| "Do you have US FDA?" | "We hold CE, ISO 13485:2016 and KGMP. We do not hold FDA clearance for this line. If your market recognises CE, we are ready today." |
| "What is your price?" | "It depends on volume and territory, and I would rather give you a real number than a booth number. Give me your annual volume and I will send indicative pricing within 48 hours." |
| "Just send me a catalogue." | "Everything is on one page - let me put it in your inbox right now with the products you looked at, so it does not sit in a folder." Then go straight to the close. |
| "Who else do you work with in my country?" | "I would not discuss another partner's territory with you, the same way I would not discuss yours with them. Tell me your territory and I will tell you whether it is open." |

### The close - always the same

> "Let me put what you looked at into an email so you have it on the plane."

Hand them your phone with the page open at the shortlist. They tick the products, type their name
and email, and tap. The enquiry lands in your inbox with the SKUs they chose, in their own words -
better than any business card.

If they will not touch your phone, take their card and say:

> "I will send this tonight, not next week."

Then actually do it that night.

### When it is not a fit

Booth time is the scarce resource. A polite twenty-minute conversation with someone who will never
buy costs you the six people who walked past while you were talking.

> "It sounds like we are not the right fit for what you are building - but if your range ever moves
> into orthopaedics, here is my card. Enjoy the rest of the show."

Say it warmly, hand the card, turn towards the panel. Ninety seconds, not twenty minutes.

### Capture discipline

Immediately after each conversation, write three things on the back of their card:

1. Country
2. Which product they physically picked up
3. The next step you promised, and by when

Do it before the next visitor, not at the hotel. By 18:00 you will not remember which of forty
people said what, and an unlabelled card is a dead lead.

### End of each day

Twenty minutes, every evening, before dinner:

- Split the cards into three piles: send tonight, send after the show, no action
- Send the "tonight" pile before you sleep - a reply that arrives while they are still in Singapore
  is worth five that arrive next week
- Count how many cards you collected. If day two is lower than day one, change where you are
  standing

## 4. Post-show follow-up (within 48 hours)

This one keeps its blanks, and it should. The whole point is that it proves you remember the
conversation - a generic follow-up after a face-to-face meeting is worse than no follow-up.

> Dear [NAME],
>
> Thank you for stopping by stand 2M01 on [DAY]. As promised:
>
> - [PRODUCT] specification and IFU - attached
> - CE certificate, ISO 13485, KGMP and Declaration of Conformity - attached
> - Indicative pricing for [VOLUME] units - attached
>
> You mentioned [THE SPECIFIC THING THEY SAID]. On that: [ONE SENTENCE ANSWER].
>
> The next step from our side would be a sample shipment of [PRODUCT] so your surgeons can handle
> it. If you send me the consignee details and your import licence number, we can have it out this
> week.
>
> Best regards,
>
> Jason Cho
> International Sales | Meta Biomed Co., Ltd.
> jasoncho@meta-biomed.com | +82-43-711-8505

**What a filled-in version looks like:**

> Thank you for stopping by stand 2M01 on Thursday. As promised: SurgyBone specification and IFU,
> our CE and ISO 13485 certificates, and indicative pricing for 500 units.
>
> You mentioned your HTO volume is growing but the 13 mm wedge is hard to source. On that: 13 x 45
> and 13 x 35 are both standard stock items for us, so that size is not a special order.
>
> The next step would be a sample shipment of the wedges so your surgeons can handle them. Send me
> the consignee details and your import licence number and we can have it out this week.

**Rule:** one specific thing they said, one specific next step, one specific ask. Never "let me know
if you need anything else."
